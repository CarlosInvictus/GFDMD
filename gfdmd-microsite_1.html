About the forum

The first global forum on disability-responsive migration and refugee governance

Migrants and refugees with disabilities remain marginalised within mainstream migration and refugee governance. The intersection of disability and migration is insufficiently understood and inadequately addressed across policy, programming and global review processes.

People with disabilities face compounding barriers: legal status, the medicalisation and stigmatisation of disability, and access to regular pathways, decent work, essential services, protection and justice. These are structural barriers, not individual misfortunes — and without disability-responsive approaches to how disability is conceptualised and legislated for, they will deepen.

A newly formed transnational consortium of four disability-led and disability-focused migration networks — spanning Asia, Africa and Europe — is convening this forum to advance evidence, social dialogue and practical solutions that support CRPD-aligned implementation of the Global Compact for Migration, the Global Compact on Refugees, International Labour Standards and refugee-related initiatives.

Why now

A gap between commitment and lived reality

The Global Compact for Safe, Orderly and Regular Migration and the Global Compact on Refugees both draw on international human rights law — including the CRPD, the Migrant Workers Convention, International Labour Standards, and the 1951 Refugee Convention. Yet disability remains inconsistently integrated and routinely deprioritised across governance frameworks and operational practice.

Too often, disability is treated as a category of individual vulnerability rather than a crosscutting governance issue requiring structural reform. Coordination between migration and refugee actors stays fragmented. Disability-disaggregated data is inadequate, which weakens both evidence-informed policy and accountability in review processes.

For refugees, the journey itself can be disabling. Exposure to violence, detention, trafficking, interrupted healthcare and precarious living conditions produces new physical and psychosocial disabilities — a dynamic that refugee systems rarely recognise.

Figures drawn from ILO (2026) and consortium research. The overlap between these populations is substantial, yet remains under-recognised in policy design, data systems and global review processes.

The timing is deliberate. The twentieth anniversary of the CRPD converges with the International Migration Review Forum in May 2026, the subsequent Global Refugee Forum cycle, and the annual International Labour Conference. That convergence is a strategic window to move from recognition to implementation.

Objectives

Four interlinked aims

Central to the forum is addressing how disability discrimination and ableism become embedded in migration and asylum laws, procedures and institutions — and identifying the practical reforms that remove them.

  • Strengthen normative coherence

    Between the CRPD, the Global Compact for Migration, the Global Compact on Refugees, International Labour Standards and refugee-related initiatives.

  • Make the disabling impact visible

    Support migration and refugee duty bearers to identify and address the disabling effects of existing policies, procedures and service delivery.

  • Back OPD leadership

    Support the meaningful participation of organisations of persons with disabilities — including those led by migrants and refugees with disabilities — in governance.

  • Generate actionable measures

    Produce disability-responsive recommendations that inform future global review processes, including the IMRF and the Global Refugee Forum.

Outcomes and outputs

What the forum produces

Outcomes

A stronger shared normative and operational understanding of disability across migration and refugee governance, grounded in the CRPD and tied directly to implementation of the GCM, GCR and ILS.

Migration and refugee actors better equipped to identify and address structural barriers, discrimination and ableism — and to apply disability-responsive approaches across migration, asylum and protection frameworks.

Strengthened leadership of migrants and refugees with disabilities, and of OPDs, at global, regional and national levels.

Outputs

  • Actionable, practice-oriented recommendations supporting CRPD-aligned implementation
  • Documented examples of good practice for migration and refugee actors
  • Strengthened OPD capacity and preparedness for future global review processes
  • A post-forum summary report and dissemination package for donors, partners and stakeholders

The consortium

Four networks. Three regions. One agenda.

This will be the first global event on disability in migration and refugee governance designed, convened and led by OPDs — including networks representing migrants and refugees with disabilities themselves. Lived experience is not consultative here; it sets the agenda.

  • Global Network of Refugees with Disabilities

    GNRD · Focal point: Julius Ntobuah

    Empowers refugee-led organisations to access advocacy opportunities at national, regional and global levels and to raise funds for their initiatives, amplifying their voices in global policy and humanitarian response.

    gnrd.ngo

  • Korean Disability Forum

    KDF · Focal point: Hanbyol Choi

    An umbrella organisation of 18 national disabled people's organisations. KDF monitors national implementation of UN human rights treaties, in particular the CRPD, and bridges DPOs across borders.

    thekdf.org

  • Disability Migration Network

    DMN · Focal points: Dr. Jun Bernandino, Karla Henson, Ben Thatcher

    An OPD-led network advancing disability-responsive migration in policy and practice, focused on the right to freedom of movement for persons with disabilities.

    disabilitymigrationnetwork.com

  • Disability and Migration Network

    DAMN · Focal point: Rebecca Yeo

    Works from the principle that immigration restrictions and inequalities are disabling and racist, and that a social model approach can build solidarity and resistance toward systemic change.

    disability-migration.org.uk

Accessibility

Participation is the point

A forum about disabling systems cannot itself be one. Access provisions are built into the forum's design and budget from the start, not added on request.

At the forum

  • Interpretation from English into French, Spanish, Swahili, Korean, Lao, Myanmar and Khmer
  • International Sign interpretation with rotating interpreters
  • Professional live captioning across all sessions
  • Accessible online hosting with technical support

On this site

Built to WCAG 2.2 AA. Everything sits on one page, with keyboard-navigable landmarks, visible focus indicators, and no motion for anyone who has asked their system to reduce it.
